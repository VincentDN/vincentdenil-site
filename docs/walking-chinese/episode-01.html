Greet, Order, Ask

Twelve sentences for a normal day, then the three ideas underneath them. Speak every line out loud. Don't read it, walk it. Tap ▶ to hear a line (uses your browser's Mandarin voice, if it has one).

No Mandarin voice found in this browser. Use a native recording for pronunciation.

How the walk works

Each block: hear the line, say it, pause. Then one short concept. No writing. If you can say it without looking, move on. Pinyin is shown as a pronunciation crutch, not the goal.

Tones in one line: ā high and flat, á rising, ǎ dip, à falling. Say it with your voice, not your head.

1. Greet

Say each line out loud after the voice. Three seconds of silence is your turn.

  1. 你好。Nǐ hǎo.Hello.
  2. 你好吗？Nǐ hǎo ma?How are you?
  3. 我很好。Wǒ hěn hǎo.I'm good.
  4. 谢谢。Xièxie.Thanks.
  5. 不客气。Bú kèqi.You're welcome.
  6. 对不起。Duìbuqǐ.Sorry.

Concept: word order, no conjugation, no "to be". Chinese sentences run subject, verb, object, like English. Verbs never change: wǒ (I), nǐ (you) and tā (he/she) all take the same verb. Adjectives don't need "am/is/are": wǒ hěn hǎo is literally "I very good". Hěn is just glue and carries almost no meaning of "very" here. Also, nǐ hǎo is pronounced ní hǎo: two third tones in a row, and the first one rises.

2. Order a drink

Same drill. Say it, then swap the noun for something you actually drink.

  1. 我要咖啡。Wǒ yào kāfēi.I want a coffee.
  2. 我不要糖。Wǒ bú yào táng.I don't want sugar.
  3. 你要茶吗？Nǐ yào chá ma?Do you want tea?
  4. 要。Yào.Yes (I do).

Concept: negation with 不, questions with 吗, answer with the verb. Put bù directly before the verb to negate it. Before another fourth tone it flips to bú, which is why you hear bú yào. To turn any statement into a yes/no question, add ma to the end, no reshuffling. There is no word for "yes": you repeat the verb. Yào means yes, bú yào means no.

3. Ask

Two questions that get you through most days.

  1. 多少钱？Duōshao qián?How much?
  2. 洗手间在哪儿？Xǐshǒujiān zài nǎr?Where is the bathroom?
  3. 我在学中文。Wǒ zài xué Zhōngwén.I'm learning Chinese.

Concept: question words stay put; tones carry meaning. English moves the question word to the front. Chinese leaves it exactly where the answer would go: zài nǎr ("is at where") is answered zài zhèr ("is at here"). And every syllable has a tone. Mā mother, má hemp, mǎ horse, mà scold: same sounds, different words. Learn the tone with the word, never separately. Zài before a verb also marks "in the middle of": wǒ zài xué is "I'm studying".

Final lap: say it without looking

Cover the English. Say each line, then answer it.

  • 你好吗？ Nǐ hǎo ma? How are you?
  • 我很好。 Wǒ hěn hǎo. I'm good.
  • 我要咖啡。 Wǒ yào kāfēi. I want a coffee.
  • 我不要糖。 Wǒ bú yào táng. I don't want sugar.
  • 多少钱？ Duōshao qián? How much?
  • 洗手间在哪儿？ Xǐshǒujiān zài nǎr? Where is the bathroom?
  • 谢谢。 Xièxie. Thanks.
